# DeepFace-B3: StyleGAN3 Face Forgery Detection via EfficientNetB3 Transfer Learning

> A binary image classification framework for distinguishing authentic human faces from AI-generated counterparts produced by StyleGAN3, leveraging EfficientNetB3 with a two-stage transfer learning strategy.

---

## Overview

The proliferation of generative adversarial networks (GANs) has significantly lowered the barrier to producing photorealistic synthetic faces, posing substantial risks to digital media integrity. This repository presents a deep learning-based detection pipeline that classifies facial images as either **Real** (authentic) or **Fake** (StyleGAN3-generated) using the **EfficientNetB3** convolutional neural network architecture pre-trained on ImageNet.

The proposed approach adopts a **two-stage training strategy**: an initial head-only training phase in which the backbone remains frozen, followed by a selective fine-tuning phase in which the final 30 layers of the backbone are unfrozen and jointly optimized at a reduced learning rate. Class imbalance is addressed through weighted loss computation, and model generalization is promoted via data augmentation and L2 regularization.

## Dataset

**Source:** [Real vs Fake Faces (StyleGAN3) — Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/troykueh/real-vs-fake-faces-stylegan3)

The dataset comprises high-resolution facial images organized into two categories: authentic photographs and synthetic faces generated by NVIDIA StyleGAN3.

```
real-vs-fake-faces-stylegan3/
├── Fake faces/        # AI-generated faces via StyleGAN3
│   ├── fake_0.png
│   ├── fake_1.png
│   └── ...
└── Real faces/        # Authentic human face photographs
    ├── real_0.png
    ├── real_1.png
    └── ...
```

### Data Partitioning

| Split      | Samples | Proportion |
| ---------- | ------- | ---------- |
| Training   | ~7,000  | 70%        |
| Validation | ~1,500  | 15%        |
| Test       | ~1,500  | 15%        |

Stratified splitting is applied to preserve class distribution across all partitions. Class imbalance is further mitigated using balanced class weights during training.

## Model Architecture

```
Input (224 × 224 × 3)
        ↓
EfficientNetB3  (ImageNet pretrained — backbone)
        ↓
GlobalAveragePooling2D
        ↓
Dense(512, ReLU) + L2(λ=0.001) + Dropout(p=0.6)
        ↓
Dense(256, ReLU) + L2(λ=0.001) + Dropout(p=0.5)
        ↓
Dense(1, Sigmoid)  →  P(Real) ∈ [0, 1]
```

### Two-Stage Training Protocol

| Stage                   | Backbone Status         | Learning Rate | Max Epochs |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------- | ---------- |
| Stage 1 — Head Training | Fully frozen            | 1 × 10⁻⁴      | 25         |
| Stage 2 — Fine-tuning   | Last 30 layers unfrozen | 1 × 10⁻⁵      | 15         |

Early stopping with best-weight restoration is applied at both stages to prevent overfitting.

| --------------- | ---------------------------------------- |

| Dropout         | p = 0.6 (Dense-512), p = 0.5 (Dense-256) |
| Early Stopping  | monitor=val_loss, patience=10            |
| LR Reduction    | factor=0.5, patience=5, min_lr=1×10⁻⁶    |

| Transformation         | Parameters                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |

> **Note:** All augmentation operations are applied _prior_ to EfficientNet-specific preprocessing (`preprocess_input`) to preserve pixel value integrity within the expected input distribution.

## Limitations and Future Research Directions

While the proposed framework demonstrates promising performance in detecting StyleGAN3-generated facial forgeries, several limitations remain that warrant further investigation.

### Current Limitations

**1. Generator-Specific Generalization**
The model is trained exclusively on faces synthesized by StyleGAN3. Its capacity to generalize to forgeries produced by alternative generative architectures — including diffusion-based models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL·E), StyleGAN2, or deepfake video extraction methods — remains unvalidated. Empirical evidence suggests that detectors trained on GAN-specific artifacts exhibit substantial performance degradation when evaluated on out-of-distribution generators (Rossler et al., 2019).

**2. Sensitivity to Post-Processing Operations**
The current pipeline does not explicitly account for common image post-processing transformations such as JPEG compression, Gaussian blurring, rescaling, or social media re-encoding. Such operations are known to suppress high-frequency forgery artifacts, potentially undermining detection reliability in real-world deployment scenarios.

**3. Limited Backbone Exploration**
The present study exclusively employs EfficientNetB3 as the feature extraction backbone. A systematic comparative evaluation against alternative architectures — such as EfficientNetB7, Vision Transformers (ViT), Xception, or domain-specific forgery detection networks — has not been conducted, leaving the optimality of the architectural choice unestablished.

**4. Absence of Explainability Analysis**
The model operates as a black-box classifier without interpretability mechanisms. The absence of attribution methods such as Grad-CAM, SHAP, or LIME precludes forensic analysis of the spatial regions and visual cues exploited by the model during classification, limiting its utility in high-stakes decision-making contexts.

**5. Static Dataset Composition**
The training set reflects a fixed snapshot of StyleGAN3 outputs. As generative models continue to evolve in fidelity and diversity, periodic dataset refreshment and model retraining may be necessary to maintain detection efficacy against emerging forgery variants.

---

### Prospective Improvements

The following research directions are proposed to address the aforementioned limitations and advance the state of the art in face forgery detection:

**Cross-Generator Robustness**
Future work should evaluate cross-generator generalization by incorporating multi-source training data encompassing diverse GAN architectures and diffusion-based generators. Adversarial training and domain adaptation techniques may further enhance model robustness to unseen forgery methods.

**Compression-Aware Training**
Integrating compression-augmented training pipelines — whereby synthetic JPEG compression artifacts are systematically introduced during data augmentation — may yield more robust representations under real-world transmission conditions.

**Advanced Backbone Architectures**
Exploration of Vision Transformer (ViT) and hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures is warranted, given their demonstrated capacity to capture long-range spatial dependencies that may encode subtle GAN-specific artifacts not readily detectable by convolutional inductive biases alone.

**Explainable AI Integration**
Incorporating gradient-based attribution methods (e.g., Grad-CAM++) or attention visualization mechanisms would provide forensic interpretability, enabling analysts to identify the discriminative facial regions — such as eye reflections, hair boundaries, or background inconsistencies — that distinguish synthetic from authentic faces.

**Temporal Consistency for Video Deepfake Detection**
Extending the framework to video-level forgery detection by incorporating temporal consistency analysis across consecutive frames represents a high-impact research direction, given the growing prevalence of deepfake video content on digital platforms.

**Hyperparameter Optimization**
A structured hyperparameter search — encompassing learning rate schedules, regularization coefficients, fine-tuning depth, and augmentation intensity — conducted via Bayesian optimization or population-based training may yield further performance gains beyond those achievable through manual tuning.
